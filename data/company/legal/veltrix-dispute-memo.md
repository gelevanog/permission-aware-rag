## Background

On 7 July 2026, Veltrix Systems, Inc. filed a complaint against Fernhill Labs Inc. in the US District Court for the Western District of Texas (Waco Division), alleging that RouteIQ's live re-planning feature infringes two of its US patents relating to "dynamic insertion of delivery stops into in-progress vehicle routes". Veltrix is a non-practising entity that acquired the patents in 2023 from a defunct telematics company. They have filed similar suits against at least four other routing vendors, two of which settled.

Our outside counsel is Harlan Pierce LLP (lead partner: Thomas Okafor).

## Our assessment

- **Validity:** we and counsel believe both patents are vulnerable. Prior art from academic vehicle routing literature (dynamic VRP papers from 1998–2004) and an open-source solver release from 2009 appear to anticipate the key claims. An inter partes review (IPR) petition is being prepared.
- **Infringement:** our live re-planning re-solves the full route rather than inserting stops into a fixed sequence, which gives us a reasonable non-infringement argument for at least one patent.
- **Exposure:** Veltrix's initial demand letter asked for a lump-sum licence. Their damages theory, if it survived, would be based on US revenue from Growth and Enterprise customers using live re-planning since 2023.
- **Litigation cost:** counsel estimates $1.8–2.6 million in fees to take the case through trial, excluding any IPR.

## Settlement authority

On 25 September 2026, the CEO and CFO approved settlement authority up to $2.35 million for a fully paid-up, worldwide licence to the Veltrix patent portfolio covering all current and future RouteIQ products, with a release of all past claims and no admission of liability. Any settlement above this figure requires board approval. Counsel will open at a much lower figure and will use the IPR filing as leverage.
